5. ILD classification – 2025 ERS/ATS framework

The 2025 ERS/ATS international multidisciplinary classification expands the classification beyond idiopathic interstitial pneumonias to include secondary causes. It distinguishes interstitial patterns from alveolar filling disorders and recognises bronchiolocentric interstitial pneumonia (BIP) as a major interstitial pattern.

Important terminology

2025 classification changes

1. Classification now incorporates both secondary and idiopathic interstitial pneumonias.

2. BIP is introduced as a major interstitial pattern, including airway-centred disease associated with hypersensitivity pneumonitis, CTD, aspiration and other causes.

3. Acute interstitial pneumonia is replaced by idiopathic diffuse alveolar damage (DAD) in the classification terminology.

4. Desquamative interstitial pneumonia (DIP) is renamed alveolar macrophage pneumonia (AMP).

5. Interstitial disorders are subclassified as fibrotic or non-fibrotic.

6. Combined and unclassifiable patterns are explicitly recognised.

7. Pattern recognition must be separated from the final multidisciplinary diagnosis.

9. HRCT pattern reference library

Diffuse cystic lung diseases

LAM: Diffuse, bilateral, relatively uniform, round, thin-walled cysts with relatively preserved intervening parenchyma. Consider angiomyolipoma and chylous manifestations.

PLCH: Upper and middle zone predominant nodules and irregular/bizarre cysts, with relative costophrenic angle sparing. Strong smoking association.

Birt–Hogg–Dubé: Variable-sized, irregular, often lentiform cysts with lower-lung, medial and subpleural predominance. Consider renal neoplasia and fibrofolliculomas.

LIP: Cysts with ground-glass opacity, nodules and septal thickening. Consider Sjögren syndrome, CTD and immunodeficiency.

Pulmonary amyloidosis / light-chain deposition: May show nodules, calcification and cystic abnormalities. Consider associated lymphoproliferative disease and Sjögren syndrome.

Cystic mimics

Emphysema: Low attenuation with absent or imperceptible walls and destruction of alveolar architecture.

Paraseptal emphysema: Subpleural low-attenuation spaces adjacent to interlobular septa; may mimic cysts.

Bullae: Focal air-containing spaces, generally greater than 1 cm, with very thin walls and adjacent emphysematous lung.

Honeycombing: Clustered, usually subpleural, thick-walled cystic air spaces in fibrotic lung, commonly with architectural distortion and traction bronchiectasis.

Pneumatoceles: Thin-walled air spaces, frequently associated with infection or trauma.

Cavities: Gas-containing spaces within nodules, masses or consolidation, usually with a definable wall.

Fibrotic interstitial lung disease patterns

UIP: Subpleural and basal predominant reticulation, traction bronchiectasis and honeycombing. Distinguish radiological pattern from the diagnosis of IPF.

NSIP: Ground-glass opacity, fine reticulation and traction bronchiectasis, often with relative subpleural sparing.

BIP: Airway-centred interstitial pattern; consider HP, CTD, aspiration, occupational exposure and drug-related causes.

PPFE: Upper-lobe pleural thickening, subpleural fibrosis, volume loss and architectural distortion.

LIP: Interstitial lymphoid disease with cysts, GGO, nodules and septal thickening.

Organising pneumonia: Patchy peripheral or peribronchovascular consolidation, sometimes with a perilobular pattern.

Important differential diagnoses and associations

PLCH – smoking, upper-lobe cysts and nodules.

LAM – diffuse cysts, renal angiomyolipoma, VEGF-D assessment.

Birt–Hogg–Dubé – FLCN mutation, familial pneumothorax, renal tumours.

LIP – Sjögren syndrome, CTD, immunodeficiency.

UIP – IPF, CTD, chronic HP, drug-associated fibrosis.

NSIP – CTD, medications, HP and idiopathic NSIP.

PPFE – idiopathic or secondary to CTD, HP, transplantation, infection and other causes.
