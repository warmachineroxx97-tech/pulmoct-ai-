📚 ILD morphological classification
Classify the morphological pattern separately from its aetiology. Consider idiopathic, connective-tissue-disease-associated, exposure-related, drug-related and other secondary causes.

Important IIP morphological patterns

UIP: Basal and subpleural fibrosis, reticulation, traction bronchiectasis and honeycombing.
NSIP: GGO, fine reticulation, traction bronchiectasis and possible subpleural sparing.
BIP: Bronchiolocentric interstitial abnormalities; consider HP, CTD, aspiration and exposures.
PPFE: Upper-lobe pleural thickening, subpleural fibrosis and volume loss.
LIP: Cysts, GGO, nodules and septal thickening; consider Sjögren syndrome.
OP: Patchy peripheral or peribronchovascular consolidation.
DAD: Acute diffuse alveolar injury, commonly with bilateral GGO and consolidation.
RB-ILD: Centrilobular nodules, GGO and airway wall thickening in smokers.
AMP: Ground-glass predominant alveolar macrophage pattern; often smoking-associated.

📖 Quick radiology reference library
LAM: Diffuse, round, relatively uniform, thin-walled cysts with preserved intervening parenchyma.
PLCH: Upper/middle-zone bizarre cysts, nodules and relative costophrenic angle sparing; smoking association.
Birt–Hogg–Dubé: Irregular, variable-sized, basal medial and subpleural cysts; consider FLCN mutation.
LIP: Cysts with GGO, nodules and septal thickening; Sjögren syndrome association.
Amyloidosis: Pulmonary nodules, calcification and possible cystic changes.
UIP: Basal subpleural reticulation, architectural distortion, traction bronchiectasis and honeycombing.
Fibrotic NSIP: GGO, reticulation, traction bronchiectasis and possible subpleural sparing.
Emphysema: Low-attenuation spaces with destruction of alveolar architecture.
Honeycombing: Clustered, usually subpleural, well-defined cystic spaces within fibrotic lung.
PPFE: Upper-lobe pleural thickening and subpleural fibrosis with volume loss.
