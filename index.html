Quick reference library

LAM: Diffuse, relatively uniform, round, thin-walled cysts; often involve the costophrenic angles. Consider in women of reproductive age, recurrent pneumothorax and renal angiomyolipoma. Serum VEGF-D ≥800 pg/mL can support diagnosis in an appropriate clinical and radiological context.
PLCH: Usually associated with cigarette smoking. Upper- and mid-lung predominant nodules, cavitating nodules and irregular or bizarre-shaped cysts; relative sparing of costophrenic angles is characteristic.
Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome: Multiple variable-sized, irregular or lentiform cysts, often lower-lung, basal, medial and subpleural; consider recurrent pneumothorax, family history and fibrofolliculomas.
LIP: Associated with Sjögren syndrome and other autoimmune disorders. May show thin-walled cysts with ground-glass opacity, centrilobular nodules and septal thickening.
Light-chain deposition disease / amyloidosis: Consider cysts with nodules, calcified nodules or monoclonal gammopathy; histopathological confirmation may be required.
UIP: Basal and subpleural predominant reticulation, traction bronchiectasis and honeycombing. Classification requires the complete HRCT pattern and clinical context.
NSIP: Often shows bilateral, relatively symmetric ground-glass opacity and fine reticulation with lower-zone predominance; subpleural sparing may be seen.
Fibrotic HP: Consider exposure history, airway-centred fibrosis, mosaic attenuation, air trapping and the three-density pattern.
PPFE: Upper-lobe pleural thickening and subpleural fibrosis, often with upper-lobe volume loss and architectural distortion.
Emphysema versus cysts: Emphysema generally has no clearly definable wall and may show central vessels within lucencies. True cysts have a definable wall and are surrounded by relatively normal lung.
Honeycombing: Stacked subpleural cystic airspaces, usually in multiple layers, in a fibrotic lung. Do not label isolated emphysematous spaces or traction bronchiolectasis as honeycombing without appropriate context.
